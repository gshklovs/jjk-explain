# Gradient descent, second lesson: how the stride (learning rate, momentum) is chosen, what a local minimum means, and how any of it relates to the answer an AI gives

[  0.0s] s1 TITLE 術式 降下・続 / Cursed Technique: Descent, Second Lesson
Cursed Technique: Descent. Second lesson. Three questions remain: the stride, the trap, and what any of it has to do with the answer the machine gives you.

[ 13.5s] s2
The terrain does not choose the stride. The user sets it before the first step, and shortens it on a schedule. Momentum carries her: each step remembers the last, so a narrow valley cannot throw her side to side.

[ 30.3s] s3
Know what the ground is. Every point on this terrain is one complete setting of the machine's weights: billions of dials. The height is how wrong its answers were, on everything it was shown.

[ 45.4s] s4
So each step is not a step. It is every dial turned slightly, so that the next answer is slightly less wrong. The path is the training. The place she stops is the machine you talk to.

[ 61.2s] s5
A local minimum is a machine that stopped improving, not one that is right. It answers with the habits it settled into. In so many dimensions true traps are rare; most are long flat saddles, and momentum is what carries her across.

[ 79.1s] s6
When you ask it a question, no descent occurs. The dials are frozen where the descent ended. What you receive is the floor of the valley she found. The sorceress smiles. Then let us hope it was a deep one.
