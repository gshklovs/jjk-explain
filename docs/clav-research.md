Patterns worth copying:
- Hook is a question in the first sentence, aimed at the viewer's pain ("Tired of ...?"), then "Bro, come
  meet the guys who ...".
- One analogy carried the whole way (door-to-door sales for clip distribution); every mechanism beat is
  a scene inside the analogy (grandma over the fence, barber mid-fade, aisle seven, a red light in Ohio).
- Numbers land as two-to-four word fragments after a full sentence: "A hundred clips. Ten thousand
  doors. That was this morning." "20 grand. Brutal."
- The echo: he repeats a phrase once, flat, with "dude": "Their own face, dude." "Mid-fade, dude."
- The verdict is one word from his vocabulary: "Billboard got mogged." "It's over for him." "That guy's a
  jester, bro."
- Self-undercut inside the pitch: "I'm not gonna lie." "Beautiful billboard, I'm not gonna lie."
- Closer is the deal in one line plus a shrug: "Flops cost you nothing. Great deal, swear to God."
- The interview register is slower, hedged ("like you said", "right?", "you know"), still blunt in the
  verdict ("they don't know what they're doing").

Takeaway for the style: the lead's brief (phone-shot talking head, big centred word groups with one
highlighted word, hard cuts to a visual the instant a concept is named) matches clav-3's framing and
clav-1's cutting; clav-1's 90s-sitcom look is a produced skit, kept as an optional flavour in prompts,
not the default lock.

## Caption rhythm to match (renderer `captions` mode "words")
- Groups of 2-4 words (measured captions run 3-8; the shorts convention and the brief say shorter), so
  the renderer packs Whisper words into groups of at most 4, breaking at punctuation.
- Each group holds from its first word's start to the next group's start (no gaps: his captions never
  blink off mid-sentence), ~0.8-1.6 s at 2.8 wps.
- One key word per group in yellow: the script can list `"keywords"` per scene; otherwise the longest
  non-stopword, and any number, is highlighted.
- Position: centred, just below the vertical centre (y ≈ 0.64 of the frame), so it sits under the mouth
  in a bust shot and over the middle of a cutaway.
