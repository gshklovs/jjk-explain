# /explain-hxh research — Hunter x Hunter (2011) narrator style

Status: complete (2026-09-02). Sections: 1 feel · 2 visual · 3 music · 4 bible · 5 lessons · 6 title card.

## 1. The feel

HxH 2011 power explanations feel like a **patient physics lecture given by someone who already knows how the story ends.** The narrator (and Wing, Bisky, Kurapika when they lecture) never hype. They state a rule, attach its cost, then reveal the exception — and the exception is always the interesting part. Nothing is free; every power is a *trade*. Numbers are used sparingly but decisively (one percentage, one count, one distance).

### Cadence rules
- **Rule → cost/condition → exception → consequence.** Almost every segment follows this shape.
- **Restriction makes power.** "The greater the restriction, the stronger the power" is the master thesis (Kurapika's chain: works on Spiders only, at the cost of his life if broken; Gon's Jajanken: announce it aloud, three visible seconds of charge, so it lands harder; Netero's vow of gratitude: one strike = years of prayer; Kastro's double: 100% accurate, but a Conjurer's copy is a memory-bleeding waste for an Enhancer).
- **The "however…" turn.** The first half of a statement is reassuring; the pivot removes the reassurance. Present tense.
- **Percentages as ranking.** The Hatsu category diagram: your own category 100%, adjacent 80%, two steps away 60%, opposite 40% (Specialist sits at 0% for the far ends unless born to it). The narrator reads it like a spec sheet, not a fun fact.
- **Naming the four principles as a ladder.** Ten (keep aura in), Zetsu (shut it off), Ren (amplify it), Hatsu (shape it). Each defined in one sentence, then immediately its failure mode ("If he releases Ten now, the aura will leak out and he will tire").
- **Chimera Ant register.** Narrator dominates, stating what a character thinks and what will happen *before* it happens. Clinical, present tense, oddly compassionate. "He has already decided. He does not yet know it."
- **Greed Island register.** Itemized. Card slot numbers, card limits, ranks (S/A/B…), number of copies in existence, transformation rules. Read like the rulebook, with a dry aside about what it implies strategically.

### Anchor lines (paraphrased cadence, not quotes)
1. "The greater the restriction placed on oneself, the greater the power gained in return."
2. "Ten keeps the aura close to the body. Release it, and the aura simply leaks away."
3. "Against his own category, an Enhancer can reach one hundred percent. Two steps away, only sixty."
4. "He announces the attack aloud. That delay is not a weakness. It is the price that buys the strength."
5. "However — the chain works only on members of the Spider. Should he use it on anyone else, he dies."
6. "The ability is flawless. That is precisely the problem: perfection costs memory."
7. "Every card has a limit. Once that number of copies exists in the game, no more can be obtained."
8. "Card number 2, 'Blessing of the Angel' — rank S, limit 3 in the world."
9. "At this moment, he is not thinking of victory. He is thinking of what he will lose by winning."
10. "It will take three seconds to charge. In three seconds, an opponent of this level can strike eleven times."
11. "A vow is not a rule imposed from outside. It is a rule one chooses. That is why it holds."
12. "This is what a Hunter does. He observes, he waits, and then — only then — he acts."

## 2. Visual vocabulary

What the show actually does while the narrator talks:
- **Freeze + diagram overlay.** Action stops on a held frame; a flat schematic (arrows, dotted circles, labels) is drawn over or beside it. Characters do not move; the diagram does.
- **Aura as a glowing outline.** A soft white/pale-blue halo hugging the silhouette, with a faint heat-haze wobble. Ten = tight outline; Ren = outline swells 2-3x with rising particles; Zetsu = outline gone, character drawn slightly desaturated.
- **Chalkboard / paper schematic panels.** Cream or slate panels with hand-drawn line diagrams, a single accent color (orange or red) marking the "active" element. The Nen category hexagon: six nodes around a ring, one highlighted, percentage labels on the neighbors.
- **Characters posing while the lecture runs.** Teacher standing, student seated; hands demonstrate (a glass of water on a leaf = water divination), a slow pan across the pose.
- **Split panels.** Vertical 2-up or 3-up: "what he is doing" / "what he is thinking" / "what will happen". Thin black gutters.
- **Close-ups on hands and eyes.** Fingers forming a fist that glows; an eye with the pupil narrowing; sweat bead in slow motion.
- **Sepia / monochrome flashback insets.** Small rounded-rectangle inset in a corner, tinted sepia or blue-grey, showing the earlier moment the rule refers to.
- **On-screen labels.** Bold sans-serif captions with a thin outline sliding in from the edge: category names, percentages, counts, ranks.
- **Recap-style "diagram of the fight".** Top-down figure icons with arrows and a dotted range circle; a timer digit in the corner.

### Style lock (paste into every video prompt; never names the show/studio/characters/arcs)
"2010s Japanese TV anime look, clean bright cel shading, thin dark linework, soft painterly watercolor backgrounds, saturated natural palette (forest greens, warm skin, sky blue), large expressive eyes, simple rounded character designs, 24fps hand-drawn feel, gentle camera pans, no CGI, no photorealism. Calm daylight lighting."
Negative: "3d render, photoreal, western cartoon, grim dark palette, heavy black shadows, motion blur."

## 4. Bible material

### Segment beats (60-80 s, 6-8 scenes)
1. **Hook / freeze** (5 s): a character mid-action, then freeze. Narrator: one sentence naming what we are about to understand. Title card follows.
2. **The rule** (8-10 s): one sentence, declarative, present tense. Diagram overlay draws the rule as a shape (outline, ring, arrow).
3. **The demonstration** (10 s): the running example acts it out on screen; the narrator describes what the character is doing and *why*.
4. **The condition / cost** (8-10 s): "However…" — the price. The diagram gains a red element (a gap, a leak, a timer).
5. **The exception** (8-10 s): the case where the rule bends, and why that case exists. Often the smartest character already knew.
6. **The number** (5-8 s): exactly one figure, stated flatly, on-screen as a label. (One number per video. Two dilutes it.)
7. **The consequence** (8-10 s): what this rule forces everyone to do. The takeaway, phrased as a strategy, not a summary.
8. **Unfreeze** (3-5 s): action resumes; the character does the thing the narrator predicted. No joke beat; HxH ends on quiet confidence.

### Registers
- **`hxh-lecture` (default).** Teacher-and-student framing. Patient, warm, slightly formal. Second-person allowed through the teacher ("Now try holding it. Good."). Diagram-heavy, daylight, cream panels. Music: default lecture bed. Sound lines: "soft room tone, birds outside", "wind through grass", "quiet piano".
- **`hxh-ominous`.** Chimera-Ant narrator. Present tense, clinical, states inner thoughts and future outcomes as facts ("He will not survive this. He knows."). Longer sentences with short verdicts. Dim palette (violet/green/grey), dust in light shafts, split panels of "thinking / doing / outcome". Music: ominous bed. Sound lines: "low drone", "distant dripping", "insect hum", "wind in a dead forest".
- **`hxh-rules`.** Game-master voice reading a rulebook. Itemized, numbered, every noun capitalized like a card name. "Card No. 7. Rank B. Limit 40." Cards / slots / binders as the diagram (a 3x3 grid of card slots filling in). Bright, playful palette, a tiny chime per item. Music: rules bed. Sound lines: "paper shuffle", "soft chime", "outdoor plaza ambience".

### Narration style rules
- Present tense. Third person for the narrator; the teacher may speak in second person in `hxh-lecture`.
- Rule sentence ≤ 14 words. Then a longer sentence explaining it. Then "However…".
- Every power gets a cost. If the concept has no cost, find its restriction (bandwidth, memory, time, coordination) and make that the cost.
- One number per video, said once and shown once.
- Never hype adjectives ("insane", "overpowered"). Use "precise", "efficient", "costly", "decisive".
- End a scene on the shortest sentence in it.

### Sound-line vocabulary (ambience only; no music words, music is mixed in post)
"soft room tone", "birds outside", "wind through grass", "distant waterfall", "quiet footsteps on wood", "paper shuffle", "soft chime", "low drone", "distant dripping water", "insect hum", "wind in a dead forest", "crowd murmur far away", "leaves rustling", "single water drop".

### Vow/restriction mapping for tech concepts (the HxH "temperature" knob)
- Rule = invariant. Cost = resource consumed. Condition = precondition/guard. Exception = the edge case the design exists for. Vow = a self-imposed constraint that buys a guarantee (e.g. "never accept a stale term" buys safety; "always halve on loss" buys fairness).


## 5. Five lesson ideas

1. **TCP congestion control** (`hxh-lecture`). Puzzle: two senders on one link; who yields, and how do they agree without talking? Diagram = the aura outline as the *congestion window*: it swells by one square each round (slow start doubles: outline doubles), then on a lost packet the outline is cut in half — the diagram literally halves. Number: **cut the window in half on loss** (the 0.5 multiplicative decrease). Takeaway: "Grow slowly, retreat instantly. Fairness comes from the retreat, not the growth."
2. **Cycloidal actuators** (`hxh-lecture`). Puzzle: how can a motor spin 30 times and the arm move once, with no backlash? Diagram = a lobed disc rolling inside a ring of pins; each pin it passes advances the output by one lobe. The overlay draws the eccentric circle and highlights the one lobe in contact. Number: **reduction ratio = number of lobes (e.g. 29:1)**. Takeaway: "The disc never jumps a tooth; the ratio is baked into the count of lobes, not the size of gears."
3. **Gradient descent** (`hxh-lecture`, ends `hxh-ominous`). Puzzle: a blindfolded climber must reach the valley floor; she can only feel the slope under her feet. Diagram = a contour map; the step vector is an arrow whose length is the slope times a scalar. Cost: too large a step and she overshoots (the arrow crosses the valley and lands higher). Number: **learning rate 0.01**. Takeaway: "Direction is free. The step size is the vow."
4. **Raft consensus** (`hxh-rules`). Puzzle: five guards must agree on one order even if two are asleep. Diagram = five card slots; a term number card sits above; a proposal advances only when it fills **3 of 5** slots (majority). Exception: a guard with a stale term card is refused. Number: **3 of 5**. Takeaway: "Majority is the only rule; the term number is the vow that makes it safe."
5. **Garbage collection** (`hxh-ominous`). Puzzle: a city of objects; who is still alive? The narrator walks from the roots, marking each object it can reach (mark), then everything unmarked disappears in one sweep. Diagram = a graph where reachable nodes glow, unreachable nodes fade to grey and vanish. Cost: the world freezes while marking (stop-the-world). Number: **one pause per cycle, e.g. 10 ms**. Takeaway: "Nothing is deleted. Things are simply no longer reachable — and that is the same thing."


## 6. Title-card look (ffmpeg drawtext)

- **bg**: `#F4ECD8` (warm cream paper) for `hxh-lecture` / `hxh-rules`; `#1B1F2A` (ink navy) for `hxh-ominous`.
- **ink** (kanji): `#1E1E1E` on cream; `#E8E4D8` on navy.
- **english** (subtitle line): `#C8501E` (burnt orange accent) on cream; `#8FA3C8` (dusty blue) on navy.
- **glyph**: `念` (Nen) large, centered, ~40% of frame height, drawn with a serif/mincho font (e.g. Hiragino Mincho ProN W6 on macOS: `/System/Library/Fonts/ヒラギノ明朝 ProN.ttc`, fallback Noto Serif CJK). Alternates per register: `誓` (vow) for ominous, `札` (card) for rules.
- **kanji convention**: two space-separated groups, *category* then *name*, in mincho ~14% frame height below the glyph, e.g. `念能力 発` (Nen ability · Hatsu), `制約と誓約 鎖` (restriction & vow · chain), `念系統 強化系` (category · Enhancer), `規則 第七番` (rule · number seven). Then the English line in a clean sans (Helvetica Neue Light / Inter) at ~5% height, tracked +8%.
- **film grain**: yes, light — `noise=alls=8:allf=t+u` over the card, plus a very slight vignette (`vignette=PI/5`). Add a 0.4 s fade-in on the glyph and a thin horizontal rule (`drawbox`, ink color, 2 px, 40% width) between kanji and English.
- **duration**: 2.5 s; hold silent except the music bed and a single soft chime on the cut in `hxh-rules`.

## 8. Do / don't for the script writer
- Do open on a puzzle the diagram will later resolve. Don't open on the definition.
- Do let the diagram BE the mechanism (window = outline size; majority = filled slots). Don't use a diagram as decoration.
- Do state what the character is thinking as fact in `hxh-ominous`. Don't do it in `hxh-lecture` (the teacher asks instead).
- Do use "However." as its own sentence once per video. Don't use it twice.
- Do end scenes on a short verdict. Don't end on a question.
- Never put the show/studio/character/arc names, or the word "Nen", into a video prompt; the doc and title-card kanji may use them.
